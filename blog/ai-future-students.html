What talking about AI with future students taught me about education.

In October 2024, during Universidad Técnica Federico Santa María’s Open House, I spoke with future students about innovation, artificial intelligence and the skills they will need in a world where access to information is no longer the main problem.

For a long time, a significant part of education was built around accessing, organizing and remembering information. Artificial intelligence is starting to change that logic in a pretty profound way.

Today we can summarize texts, explore ideas, generate alternatives or find answers in seconds. That does not make learning less important. It makes it more important to understand what to do with all that capability.

That was one of the ideas I wanted to put on the table when I had the chance to give a talk during the 2024 Open House at Universidad Técnica Federico Santa María.

In front of students thinking about what to study and what their professional future might look like, the conversation quickly moved away from “what can AI do?” and toward something much more interesting: what skills will matter when these tools are available to everyone.

01 / CRITICAL THINKING

Critical thinking before fast answers.

AI can produce an answer in seconds. But it does not necessarily know what the right question is.

That is why, as these tools become more capable, it becomes more valuable to know how to evaluate information, detect assumptions, compare alternatives and build your own judgment.

The educational challenge should not be to compete with a machine on speed. It should be to prepare people to decide which answers actually make sense.

The advantage will not come from having access to AI. It will come from knowing what to do with it.
02 / CREATIVITY

Creativity becomes more important, not less.

There is a fairly common view of AI as a technology that “does things for us.”

I find it more useful to see it as a technology that expands what we can explore.

A student can test an idea, build a prototype, visualize something that previously required specialized tools or explore a problem from different angles much faster.

That lowers the cost of experimentation. And when experimentation becomes cheaper, creativity depends less on the resources available and more on the ability to imagine what is worth building.

03 / CAREERS

Careers are going to change too.

One of the things that stood out most in that conversation was how interested students were in understanding how these technologies could connect with the careers they were considering.

And that is probably a better question than trying to guess which jobs AI is going to “replace.”

Engineering, business, design, education, healthcare or almost any discipline will incorporate new tools and new ways of working.

The opportunity is not only in learning how to use AI. It is in combining domain knowledge with technology to solve problems in ways that were not possible before.

04 / EDUCATION

Education is not preparation for a static world.

Looking back on that talk, there is one idea I still find relevant.

We often educate as if we could predict exactly which tools, processes and jobs a person will encounter ten years later. It is becoming increasingly clear that we cannot.

Maybe that is why the role of education needs to shift a little: less preparation for executing one specific way of working, and more development of the ability to learn, question, create and adapt.

Artificial intelligence accelerates that shift, but it did not create it.

05 / TAKEAWAYS

What stayed with me.

That conversation with future students reminded me of something simple: people entering university today will probably work with tools that do not exist yet.

We cannot teach them all of those tools. But we can help them build the skills that will allow them to learn them when they appear.

  • Critical thinking to evaluate information and make better decisions.
  • Creativity to explore possibilities that were not viable before.
  • Curiosity to keep learning as the tools change.
  • The ability to connect disciplines, problems and technology.

Technology changes. Those capabilities become even more important.
