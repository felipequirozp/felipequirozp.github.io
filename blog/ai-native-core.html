The next product leap is built with AI at the core

At AI Native Startups, teams from Buk, Vambe and AgendaPro shared how AI is moving from experiment to real product and business infrastructure.

Felipe Quiroz Product, Growth & Operations
August 2026 · Santiago, Chile
AI Native Startups · Platanus × AWS
In this note
01 / AI-native is not adding a chatbot02 / From experiment to infrastructure03 / What changes in product04 / What I took away

The idea I kept from AI Native Startups was simple: the difference is not whether you use AI, but how deeply it changes the way you build the product.

Listening to teams like Buk, Vambe and AgendaPro made one transition very visible: moving from isolated experiments to capabilities that become part of the business infrastructure.

AI-native is not a feature category. It is a different way to build.

01 / AI-native is not adding a chatbot

Adding a conversational interface on top of an existing product can be useful, but it does not necessarily make a company AI-native.

The more interesting shift happens when AI changes the workflow, architecture, or even what part of the work the software performs.

A useful question

What part of the product would look radically different if this capability had existed from day one?

02 / From experiment to infrastructure

Experimentation proves possibility. Integration requires something much harder: reliability, evaluation, cost control, security, adoption, and an operation capable of sustaining it.

At that point, the conversation is no longer only technical. Product, engineering, and business have to decide together where AI actually creates leverage.

03 / What changes in product

When AI enters the core, product teams have to think differently. Outputs are less deterministic, evaluation matters more, and teams have to make new choices about user control.

Speed changes too. Small teams can prototype, test hypotheses, and learn much faster than before.

04 / What I took away

The next product leap may come less from adding traditional features and more from redesigning entire workflows around new capabilities.

The advantage will not simply come from “using AI”, but from learning how to integrate it in a way that makes the product more useful, the team faster, and the business harder to replicate.
