My everydaystack.

Five tools I use to organize ideas, think through problems, turn concepts into prototypes and keep projects moving.

My everyday stack · Five apps I use to think, organize and build

A good stack keeps ideas moving. These five apps help me go from scattered thoughts to something organized, tested and ready to keep building.

My everyday stack is not about having the perfect setup. It is about moving from an idea to something useful without losing context along the way. These are five apps I use to think, organize, build and ship.

Notion keeps the context together.

Notion is where I give projects a home. Notes, plans and loose ideas become easier to revisit when they live somewhere I can organize and connect. It helps me keep the context close while the work moves forward.

Claude helps me work through the first draft.

I use Claude as a thinking partner when I want to explore an idea, work through a longer piece of writing or get a first pass on a complex task. The useful part is the back-and-forth: a prompt starts the work, and clear judgment shapes the result.

ChatGPT helps me move from question to output.

ChatGPT helps me move quickly between questions and useful outputs, from clarifying a thought to outlining, researching and producing a first version. It is one of the fastest ways to get unstuck and keep a project moving.

Lovable makes ideas easier to see.

Lovable makes it easier to turn a rough concept into a working prototype. Seeing an idea on a screen changes the conversation: you can react to a real flow, improve it and test what feels right.

GitHub keeps the work moving.

GitHub is where the work becomes durable. It keeps code, websites and changes organized, so ideas can move from a prototype into something people can actually use.

One app rarely changes the way I work. The value comes from a stack that lets each tool do its part—and lets me keep moving.

These five tools do different jobs, but together they make my day more fluid: organize context, think through the problem, create a first version and keep the work moving. The tools may change; using them with intention is what matters.
