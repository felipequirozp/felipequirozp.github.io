The challenge

Modernize without losing what makes engineering rigorous.

The question was not simply how to add new technologies to a curriculum. It was how an engineering university could evolve its academic model, capabilities and institutional processes for a changing technological and industrial landscape.

The work connected external signals — emerging technologies, international education models, interdisciplinary learning and evolving industry needs — with the realities of a complex university system. The goal was to identify where transformation could create real value and translate those signals into concrete institutional opportunities.

What I worked on

From global signals to actionable institutional choices.

01 · Benchmarking

Look beyond the institution.

Conducted national and international benchmarking to identify emerging models, best practices and strategic opportunities in engineering education, innovation and university-industry collaboration.

02 · Emerging capabilities

Map what future engineers will need.

Developed recommendations for integrating emerging technologies, interdisciplinary capabilities and new learning experiences into academic programs without treating innovation as a standalone layer.

03 · Operating model

Turn ambition into systems.

Designed improvements to academic processes, institutional initiatives and operating models so strategic priorities could move from concept into repeatable execution.

04 · Stakeholder alignment

Translate strategy into action.

Worked with faculty and institutional stakeholders to connect long-term transformation goals with concrete initiatives, tradeoffs and implementation paths.

Approach

Strategy as a bridge between evidence and execution.

1. Scan

Understand global shifts in engineering, education, technology and industry.

2. Synthesize

Translate signals into strategic implications for programs, capabilities and institutional design.

3. Activate

Shape recommendations that could be discussed, prioritized and converted into initiatives.

Why it mattered

Transformation is not a technology project.

The strongest outcome was a clearer way to think about institutional change: not as isolated initiatives, but as a connected system of curriculum, capabilities, processes, incentives and relationships with the outside world.

The project contributed to a broader transformation agenda connecting engineering education, innovation, technology and evolving industry needs — while keeping the focus on what could realistically be implemented inside a large academic institution.
