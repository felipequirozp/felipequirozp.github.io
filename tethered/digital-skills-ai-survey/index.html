The challenge

Before designing AI policy, we needed to understand what was already happening inside schools.

Access to technology alone does not tell you whether a student can search, evaluate an answer, use AI critically or ask for support when something does not make sense.

The project set out to build a more useful baseline for understanding students’ relationship with the digital world. The survey combined questions on access, digital skills, habits and use of AI tools, making it possible to look across grade levels and schools.

The study was implemented with SLEP del Pino across San Bernardo, El Bosque, La Pintana and Calera de Tango, connecting research, field deployment and school-level reporting.

What we measured

More than “using technology”: access, judgment and experience with AI.

The diagnostic was designed to observe multiple layers of students’ digital experience and turn them into information that schools and system leaders could act on.

01 / Access

Digital conditions

Which devices and resources students have available and the conditions in which they use them.

02 / Skills

Navigation and judgment

How students describe their ability to search, understand and operate in digital environments.

03 / AI

Use and usefulness

How students use AI tools, where they find them useful and how they interpret their answers.

04 / School

Expected support

What kind of support students want from schools as new digital tools and challenges emerge.

Implementation

A study that had to work in real school conditions, not just in a spreadsheet.

At this scale, the diagnostic required coordination across schools, devices, survey administration and participation follow-up. The goal was not simply to collect answers, but to produce comparable evidence that individual schools could actually use.

ConsiliumBots connected the measurement layer and results experience; from Tether Education, the work was tied to territorial implementation, coordination with the public education system and deployment across schools.

Research agenda

From one local diagnostic to a broader research agenda.

The public results cover the 2025 Santiago study. The project also opened a path for future measurement waves and collaboration with schools and research partners.

The project’s broader agenda includes planned follow-up in Colombia and the Dominican Republic, creating opportunities to compare contexts and keep building evidence on digital skills and AI adoption in education.
