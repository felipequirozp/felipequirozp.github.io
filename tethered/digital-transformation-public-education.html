Strategy · Implementation · Digital Admissions

The challenge.

Digitizing a public process is not simply moving a form from paper to a screen. It means understanding rules, actors, operations and different institutional contexts, then getting all of them to work through shared infrastructure.

In this project, I worked with SLEP del Pino and CORMUN Rancagua on initiatives aimed at modernizing admissions processes and expanding access to digital application tools.

The challenge was twofold: translating institutional and operational needs into a viable implementation, while also enabling schools, leadership teams and public-sector stakeholders to adopt the process consistently.

From institutional requirements to implementation.

A major part of the work involved coordinating actors with different needs, timelines and responsibilities. That meant understanding how public systems operated, identifying constraints and turning them into concrete implementation decisions.

My role sat between institutions, school leadership and internal product teams, helping translate public and operational requirements into strategies that could be executed at scale.

In public education, implementation does not happen after the product. It is part of the product.

My role.

I led strategic and implementation initiatives with SLEP del Pino and CORMUN Rancagua, coordinating public stakeholders, school leadership and internal product teams.

My focus was on building bridges between institutional needs and execution: understanding requirements, organizing priorities, aligning stakeholders and translating those signals into scalable implementation strategies.

More than deploying a tool, the work was about creating the conditions for digital infrastructure to become part of real admissions processes inside complex public systems.

How we approached it

Institutions, product and execution in one system.

The project required cross-functional work: understanding each institution's reality, supporting the teams that would operate the process and maintaining a close connection with product to resolve friction and adapt the implementation.

What we were doing

Digitizing processes that still depended too heavily on paperwork.

We automated applications, waitlist management, admission offers and digital enrollment, reducing manual paperwork, increasing transparency, saving administrators time and improving capacity utilization across the network.

Impact

More than 100 schools inside a shared transformation.

The initiatives reached more than 100 public schools across systems serving more than 35,000 students.

Scale did not depend on technology alone. It depended on coordinating actors, adapting implementation to institutional context and building processes that could last beyond a pilot.

What I learned.

This project reinforced how I think about digital transformation: implementing technology in complex systems requires organizational design and operations just as much as product.

It also showed me the value of translating between different worlds. Public-sector teams, schools and product teams do not always speak the same language, and an important part of the work is building that connection.

When that translation works, technology stops being an extra layer and starts becoming real infrastructure for operating better.
