The project

Replacing the queue with a digital registry.

The regularization stage had historically required families to approach schools directly in search of available seats.

For the 2024 enrollment cycle, Anótate en la Lista moved that process into a shared digital registry. Families could register interest remotely, while schools could manage requests and available vacancies through a common system rather than relying on physical lines and fragmented local processes.

This case study focuses on the original 2023–24 implementation. Families looking for current information should use the Ministry of Education's official service channels.

How it worked

One digital layer connecting families, waitlists and available seats.

01 / Apply remotely

Request vacancies online.

Families could register interest in public and subsidized private schools without going physically to each institution.

02 / Manage waitlists

A shared digital process.

Schools gained a common workflow to register requests, manage waiting lists and respond to available capacity.

03 / Preserve order

Registration order mattered.

Offers were supported by the order in which requests were registered and by the admissions rules applicable to the process.

Implementation

A public-service experience designed to work in real conditions.

The value was not only moving a form online. The project created a clearer operating layer between families and schools at a moment when information, timing and vacancy availability were changing quickly.

That meant designing for simplicity on the family side while preserving traceability and operational consistency for schools and the broader admissions system.
