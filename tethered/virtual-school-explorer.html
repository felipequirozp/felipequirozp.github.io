The challenge.

Choosing a school is a high-impact decision, but the information needed to make it is often scattered, difficult to compare and not always designed around the questions families actually need to answer.

Virtual School Explorer was designed to transform that experience. Instead of forcing families to navigate multiple sources of information, the platform brought educational options into a map-based interface and let them explore schools by location, grade level, cost, academic model and other needs.

The goal was to bring more transparency to available options and make complex processes—such as understanding costs, open seats or admission probabilities—much easier to navigate.

From search tool to guidance platform.

The product went far beyond displaying schools on a map. Families could begin by applying filters or interacting with an AI assistant. From those signals, the platform could deliver personalized information, adjust the search and recommend options based on each family’s needs.

A richer way to understand each school.

Each institution could have a digital profile that went far beyond a traditional listing. Families could understand the school’s educational approach, explore spaces through virtual tours, watch community testimonials and access multimedia content about the school and its surroundings.

That combination helped close the gap between finding a school on a list and actually understanding whether it could be a good fit for a family.

The value was not just in showing more information, but in turning complex information into clearer decisions.

My role.

From Partnerships, I worked on connecting Virtual School Explorer with the different actors across the education ecosystem: schools, families, education communities, Local Public Education Services and other institutions connected to education.

My focus was on opening doors, building strategic relationships and translating the product’s value for different stakeholders, helping the platform reach more communities and be understood not only as a technology tool, but as a new way to support education decisions.

That work meant moving between product, institutions and local contexts: understanding each actor’s needs, identifying collaboration opportunities and building partnerships that could expand TetherEd’s reach and bring the product into real-world use cases.

Impact

The product needed relationships to reach scale.

Through partnerships, we reached more than 20,000 families in 2024 and connected with more than 100 public schools in Chile.

More than distributing a platform, the challenge was to build the relationships needed for it to reach families and education communities within a system where adoption depends as much on the product as it does on trust between actors.

What I learned.

Virtual School Explorer reinforced an idea that later became central to how I think about product and growth: in complex markets, launching a strong solution is not enough. You also have to design how that solution enters the system.
